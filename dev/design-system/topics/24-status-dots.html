Topic 24: telling "waiting" from "unset"

blockr.dock puts an 8px dot on a block's mark for four states (block_status_style()). Two of them were already two near-identical ambers, #f59e0b and #eab308; the consistency pass made both border-warning, so today they are the same dot.

They ask for different things. Unset ("Set this block's inputs") means this block needs you: a required field is empty, and that field carries the same amber cue. Waiting ("Waiting for a data input") means one of its inputs is not ready: nothing is linked in, or the block upstream is itself unset, waiting or failed (blockr.core, block_eval_status()). There is nothing to do on this block. The label is the dot's tooltip and the block's status line.

Proposal: B, waiting is a hollow grey ring. Waiting passes down the chain: an empty read block makes every block below it wait, so today the whole chain is amber and the one block that needs you is lost among blocks that only wait for it. Below a failed block the same holds, with one red dot above a column of amber ones. With B, amber marks only that one block, and the blocks below it wait in grey. Stale stays a solid grey dot: it has run, just not since the last change. The token is --blockr-dock-status-waiting, pointing at text-muted, drawn as a ring. Runner-up: C.
