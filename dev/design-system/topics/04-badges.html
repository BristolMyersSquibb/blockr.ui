Topic 4: badges, tags and buttons, told apart

Your notes on B1 and B2: the idea behind the four kinds was unclear, you found "one chip" prettier, and you like square badges but worry they look like buttons. The idea, in one line: a badge is something you only read ("Categorical", "blockr.dplyr", "Added"), a tag is a value you picked (a column in a select, with ×), a pill changes a setting when clicked (the >= operator), and a count is a number on a button. The real question is what tells a reader "you can click this": shape, border, or nothing but colour. Three systems, each on the same strip of blockr. Buttons are the decided ones in every option.

Q1What tells a badge from something you can click

Proposal: C, square everywhere, and a border means you can click it. You keep the square badges you like, and they still cannot be mistaken for a button, because buttons and pills have an edge and badges and tags are only a fill. It is also the quietest of the three: the many read-only labels in a column list or a block browser lose their outline. Tags need no border because they sit inside a field that is itself the control; their × is the click. The names stay (badge, tag, pill, count) because the code needs one class per job, even where two of them look alike. Runner-up: B, where round ends mark what you only read.

Recorded as they are

  • Colour on a badge only for status (danger, warning, success) and for "on" (accent); types and packages are neutral (B4).
  • A count is a 16px capsule in the tint of its button (B3); the reset button carries the filter state (B5).
  • A tag's × shows on the hovered tag only (B6).
  • Badges are 18px with 11px text; tags and pills 24px.
