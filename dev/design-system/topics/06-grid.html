Topic 6: the grid for fields

Your two points: a select needs more room than a number, and having a three-column section above a four-column section looks wrong. Three ways to lay out the same eight fields in two sections. Set the panel width with the buttons, or drag the bottom-right corner of any panel. "Show columns" draws the grid's tracks.

Panel width

A. Two ladders (the L4 proposal)

Selects on a wide ladder (4 / 3 / 2 / 1 columns), numbers and flags on a narrow one (4 / 3 / 2 / 1 at other widths). Every field in a section is the same width. The two sections step at different widths, so their columns do not line up.

B. One grid, two field sizes

One set of columns for the whole block, each about 130px wide; a wider panel gets more of them (8 / 6 / 4 / 2 / 1). A number or a checkbox takes one column, a select or a text field two, and something long (tags, an expression) the whole row. Columns line up across sections at every width. A section with few fields leaves room on the right instead of stretching them.

C. One grid, one field size

The wide ladder for everything. Columns line up across sections, but a number or a checkbox gets as much width as a select.

Proposal: B, one grid with two field sizes. The column is the same everywhere in blockr, about 130px, and a field says how many it needs: one for a number or a checkbox, two for a select or a text field, the whole row for tags or an expression. Sections line up at every width, a select never shrinks to a number's width, and a new block only has to say "small", "large" or "full" per field. The cost is space left over on the right of a short section on a wide panel. Runner-up: C, if you prefer every field filling the row over fields keeping their size.
